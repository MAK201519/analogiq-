What is the morning about?

Two questions every travel brand is now facing.

Front stage

If a holiday is planned, compared and booked inside ChatGPT, whose customer is it?

Travellers are starting, and increasingly finishing, in AI assistants. Your products are being found, described and compared by tools you don't control. The question is how to stay visible there, keep the booking, and keep the relationship once it's made.

On the morning

  • Where discovery is moving, and what an assistant shows and why.
  • How travel brands keep the booking, and the customer, when the journey starts elsewhere.
  • What this does to your website, your search budget and your data.
Back stage

How does a marketing team do more with less, and still make it feel personal?

Behind the scenes, agents can take on the work that eats a team's week: joining the data, finding what's unsold, producing the content, spotting what's working. Done well, that isn't only efficiency. It's the capacity to deliver a more tailored experience at scale.

On the morning

  • Which jobs agents take on first, and which they shouldn't yet.
  • What they need from your data, and what to fix before you build.
  • How to keep people's judgement in the loop as the work scales.
Where this goes

A possible roadmap to 2030, and what to be thinking about now.

Most of the morning is spent here. Not predictions, but a view of where we think travel is heading on both sides of the stage, and the decisions that fall to a marketing team in the next two years.

  • Assistants that book, not just searchWhat changes when the journey starts and finishes in ChatGPT, and what a travel brand has to own to stay in the relationship.
  • The brand as the data behind the assistantWhy your product, pricing and availability data becomes the product, and what that does to the website.
  • Marketing teams of people and agentsWhat the team looks like when agents do the routine work and people set the rules, and how you get there without losing the judgement.
  • Knowing when not to actStale data, conflicting rules, consumer law: the agent that asks rather than guesses, and why you're responsible for it either way.
